Five red flags to check every time

Most phishing emails fail at least one of these checks.

The sender’s address

Look past the display name. Lookalike domains such as paypa1 or extra words like “-login” are a giveaway.

Pressure to act fast

“Within 2 hours” and “final warning” exist to stop you thinking. Real services give you time.

Links that don’t match

Hover before you click. If the address differs from the text, don’t open it.

Requests for secrets

No genuine service asks for your password, OTP or card details by email.

Odd attachments

Files like “form.pdf.exe” or unexpected .html and .zip files can install malware.
